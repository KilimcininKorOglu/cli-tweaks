# LinkedIn Articles

Platform-specific formatting rules for LinkedIn Articles. Apply these ON TOP of the shared rules in `../SKILL.md`.

## Platform Constraints

- No strict character limit (LinkedIn supports long-form)
- H2 and H3 headers supported
- Professional audience -- credibility signals matter
- Desktop and mobile reading (mixed)

## Subheadings

- Insert H2 subheadings every 4-7 paragraphs
- H3 allowed for sub-sections within a major section
- Subheadings can be slightly more descriptive than X (topic + angle)
  - NO: "Introduction"
  - YES: "Why most teams get onboarding wrong"
  - ALSO OK: "The data behind developer retention"
- Sentence case preferred

## Paragraph Rules

- Max 6 sentences per paragraph
- Default: 2-4 sentences for professional flow
- Single-sentence paragraphs for emphasis (2-3 per article)
- Slightly longer exposition paragraphs acceptable for data-heavy sections

## Formatting

- **Bold**: key metrics, statistics, and pivotal phrases
- **Lists**: more acceptable than X, but still strategic -- use for frameworks, checklists, or comparisons
- **Block quotes**: for real quotations or highlighted insights (pull-quote style)
- **Code blocks**: acceptable for technical audiences
- **Italics**: sparingly for book titles or terminology introduction
- **Links**: inline, relevant -- link to sources, not self-promotion

## Opening Enhancement

LinkedIn rewards credibility in the opening. After the strong opening (from shared rules), consider a brief credential signal within the first 3 paragraphs:

- "After [N] years of [relevant experience]..."
- "Having [specific achievement], I've noticed..."
- "My team at [company] faced this exact problem..."

This is NOT a bio dump -- it's one sentence of relevant context.

## Tone Guidance

LinkedIn Articles reward:
- Evidence-based arguments (data, case studies, named examples)
- Professional but not corporate (avoid jargon, use plain language)
- Actionable frameworks the reader can apply at work
- Industry-specific insights with cross-industry applicability

LinkedIn Articles penalize:
- Overly casual tone ("yo, check this out")
- Pure opinion without evidence
- Engagement bait ("Like if you agree!")
- Motivational fluff without substance

## Closing Rules

- A thoughtful CTA is acceptable (not engagement bait):
  - "What patterns have you seen in your organization?"
  - "I'd be curious to hear how other teams handle this."
  - NOT: "Like, comment, and share!"
- Optional: "About the author" mini-bio (2-3 sentences) after the article body
- Alternatively, end with a forward-looking implication or a concrete next step

## LinkedIn-Specific Elements

### The Takeaway Box

For framework or process articles, consider adding a summary box before the closing:

```markdown
**Key Takeaways:**
- [Takeaway 1 -- one sentence]
- [Takeaway 2 -- one sentence]
- [Takeaway 3 -- one sentence]
```

Max 3-5 takeaways. Each must be actionable or insightful on its own.

### Engagement Hook

LinkedIn's algorithm favors early engagement. The first paragraph should be visible in the feed preview (~300 characters). Make it compelling enough to click "See more."
