# X (Twitter) Articles

Platform-specific formatting rules for X Articles. Apply these ON TOP of the shared rules in `../SKILL.md`.

## Platform Constraints

- Soft character limit: ~10,000 characters for comfortable reading
- No H3 or deeper headers -- only H2 subheadings
- Minimal formatting -- bold and line breaks are the primary tools
- Mobile-first reading experience (most X users are on mobile)

## Subheadings

- Insert bold H2 subheadings every 3-6 paragraphs
- Subheadings are **statements or provocations**, not topic labels
  - NO: "Section 2: The Problem"
  - YES: "The traditional bargain is dead"
- Use sentence case, not title case
- Keep subheadings under 8 words when possible

## Paragraph Rules

- Max 5 sentences per paragraph (hard limit)
- Prefer 1-2 sentence paragraphs for punchiness
- Single-sentence paragraphs for emphasis (2-4 per article)
- White space is your friend -- X readers skim aggressively

## Formatting Constraints

- **Bold**: key phrases only, never full sentences, max 1-2 per section
- **Lists**: rare, 3-5 items max, each item one line
- **Block quotes**: only for real quotations from named sources
- **Code blocks**: avoid unless the article is technical
- **Italics/underlines**: do not use
- **Links**: inline sparingly, never "click here"

## Tone Guidance

X Articles reward:
- Strong opinions backed by specifics
- Personal experience as evidence
- Concise, punchy language
- Conversational authority (not academic)

X Articles penalize:
- Hedging ("it might be worth considering...")
- Corporate speak ("leveraging synergies")
- Wall-of-text paragraphs
- Excessive qualifiers

## Closing Rules

- No CTA whatsoever ("What do you think?" is forbidden)
- No "follow me for more" or engagement bait
- End with conviction -- a declarative sentence or a vivid image
- The last sentence should be quotable on its own

## Severity: Skimmability Check

After formatting, verify the article passes the "3-second skim test":
1. Read only the subheadings -- do they tell the story?
2. Read only the bold phrases -- do they convey the argument?
3. Read only the first sentence of each section -- does the logic flow?

If any test fails, restructure until it passes.
